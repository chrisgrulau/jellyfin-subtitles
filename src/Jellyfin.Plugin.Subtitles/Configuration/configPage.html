Shoal Subtitles

Finds subtitles for videos that are missing them, checks candidates against the actual audio, and fixes their timing. Every change can be undone.

Results

How it works

Subtitle files beside your films and episodes are checked daily, and subtitles are found for videos that have none in your languages (Scheduled Tasks → Shoal); new videos are handled a few minutes after they are added; if switched on, subtitles are generated for videos nothing could be found for. Corrections follow the timing setting below; every change keeps the original, and every added or generated subtitle can be removed with Undo. ▸ opens a row's details: the files, what changed, lines to review and the numbers behind the result. Hover over (or tap) an icon to see what it counts.

Times are h:mm:ss.mmm. ▶ plays that line's audio. Saving keeps the original: Undo in the results brings it back.

Restore all originals (before uninstalling)

Puts back the original of every subtitle file this plugin changed, and removes every subtitle it added or generated, as Undo would for each. A file changed since outside this page (by you or another program) is left alone and listed, as is one whose original is no longer kept. Restored files aren't changed again unless you choose Check again; if you're about to uninstall, also untick Enabled and save. Not while a scheduled task is running.

Finding, checking and generating
Searches your subtitle providers (such as the OpenSubtitles plugin), checks the best candidates against the audio, and adds one only if it fits. Existing subtitle files are never replaced.
When films or episodes are added (or a subtitle file appears beside one), their subtitles are checked and missing ones searched for a few minutes later, instead of the next night. The same limits apply (files and videos per run, downloads per day, spending; the day's new-video runs share one run's AI checks), and it waits while a scheduled task runs. Generating subtitles stays nightly.
Default 10 (1 to 1440). Each new video starts the wait over, so a whole season being filed is handled in one go.
When the search finds nothing that fits, the whole video is transcribed with the Full transcript speech-to-text below and a subtitle is made from it, only in the language spoken. It is added as <video>.<language>.generated.srt, which players list as "generated", and it's machine-made: names and quiet or overlapping speech may be wrong. The search goes on, and a real subtitle found later replaces it; Undo removes it. The built-in speech-to-text is free but slow on a CPU (roughly as long as the video, or longer); a paid service is charged per minute of audio (a two-hour film is 120 minutes) within your monthly limit. Runs nightly (Scheduled Tasks → Shoal → Generate missing subtitles).
Default 20 (0 to 200). A video with no speech to speak of (music, silence) gets no subtitle and isn't transcribed again unless the Full transcript service or model changes.
A subtitle whose timing check was unclear or settled by few matching words, or whose wording the AI audit flagged, is compared line by line with a Full transcript of its video. Lines that are heard but missing, lines with nothing heard, and lines whose names, numbers, negations or words differ from what is said are listed with what was heard and a suggested fix, for you to apply or decline one by one (or open in the editor); nothing is changed on its own. Words heard with low confidence aren't used. Only subtitles in the audio's language; generated subtitles are never checked. Check whole file in the results asks for any subtitle to be checked, even with this off. Transcripts are kept, so checking again, or generating from the same video, costs nothing more. Runs nightly with the generated subtitles (Scheduled Tasks → Shoal → Generate missing subtitles and check whole files).
Some subtitles were made for another version of the video: a scene added or cut, a recap or cold open, ad breaks, sometimes a different frame rate too. They start in time and then jump or drift part-way, so no single shift fixes them and the timing check leaves them unclear. With this on, a subtitle whose timing check was unclear, or settled with few matching words, is lined up with a Full transcript of its video section by section. If the timing jumps (for example "at 12:40 by +3.2 s and at 31:05 by −41 s"), the correction is listed for you to apply or decline; nothing is changed on its own. Lines covering a part the video doesn't have are flagged for you to remove, not moved. Subtitles for another episode or in another language are left alone. Try fixing timing by section in the results asks for one, even with this off. Transcripts are shared with the whole-file check and generated subtitles, so a video is never transcribed twice. Runs nightly with them.
Default 5 (0 to 200), shared by the whole-file check and fixes by section. Files you picked with Check whole file or Try fixing timing by section go first and count too.
Default 4 (0 to 24; 0 means no limit), shared by generating subtitles and checking whole files. Once this long has passed since the night's run began, no new video is started; one being transcribed finishes. The rest wait for the next night.

SubDL

An extra subtitle site (the successor to Subscene), searched after Jellyfin's own providers. It needs a free API key from subdl.com. Its downloads count towards the daily limit under Advanced.
PGS and VobSub tracks (common on Blu-ray and DVD rips) work, but most clients have to burn them into the video. Untick to also look for a text subtitle for those videos. Forced-only tracks (signs and foreign-language parts) never count.
Clean-up
Subtitle-site promotions anywhere, and lines like "Subtitles by …" near the start or end. Applied automatically; this can be changed under Advanced settings.
Removes [door slams], (laughs), speaker labels and music-only lines. This changes the wording, so it follows the wording setting above.

Empty lines are always removed. Repeated lines are merged and tidied into one, following the wording setting. Lines that overlap the next one, or show too briefly to read, are adjusted, following the timing setting. Every change can be undone.

Speech-to-text

Used to check subtitles against what is actually said. Built-in runs on this server with no setup (slower, free); a local service is free and fast with a GPU; cloud services are fast but charge per minute of audio.

When a new video's name doesn't say which episode it is, Ingest can ask for a transcript of two minutes of it, which the AI plugin compares with the episode synopses. Uses the Context for AI decisions service above (turn it on; Built-in is free), within the spending limits below.
A failed call is first tried again a few times (about 2, 4, 8 and 16 seconds apart). If it still fails, the check uses the local service, or else the built-in one if it is allowed and already downloaded; never a paid service. The result says so and offers Rerun with … the service you chose.

Services and keys

Keys are stored on this server in a file only Jellyfin can read. They are never shown again, logged or included in exports; you can replace or clear them. Test sends one second of near-silence (for a paid service, a small fraction of a cent).

Built-in

Runs on this server's CPU. Model base (the default; about 90 MB with the program) or small (more accurate, slower; about 200 MB), set in the Model box above. Downloaded in the background when you press Download now or Test, or the first time it's needed.
A speech-to-text service with an OpenAI-compatible API on this server or your network (for example a faster-whisper server, speaches, or the whisper.cpp server). Free, and fast with a GPU.

Deepgram

This is an Admin key. Besides transcribing, it can create and delete keys and see billing for your whole Deepgram project. It's safer to transcribe with a key that can only transcribe.

The plugin asks Deepgram for a new key with only the usage:write scope and uses it for transcription from then on. The new key is created in your Deepgram project and stays there if this plugin is removed; delete it in Deepgram's console when you no longer need it.
Reading the balance needs a Deepgram Admin or Owner key. A billing key is stored like the others (only Jellyfin can read the file), never shown again, sent only to api.deepgram.com, and used only to read the balance, never to transcribe.
Transcribing with an Admin key works, but if the key ever leaked it could be used to manage your Deepgram project. A separate billing key keeps the transcription key limited.

OpenAI

The built-in speech-to-text needs your permission before its first use. It downloads a Whisper program (whisper.cpp, built by this project) and a speech model: about 90 MB in total with the default model, about 200 MB with the small one. They come only over HTTPS from this project's GitHub releases. Each file is checked against a checksum built into the plugin before it runs, and again each time it starts. It runs on this server's CPU with low priority and a time limit, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Until you allow it, anything set to Built-in waits. A local service or a cloud provider works without this.
Spending limit

Spending limits are set in Shoal AI. Paid speech-to-text (Deepgram, OpenAI) is kept within the currency, monthly limit and per-service limits on Shoal AI's settings page. The API keys stay here.

Costs and limits are shown and set in this currency. Most providers charge in US dollars; their charges are converted using the European Central Bank's daily rates. If current rates can't be fetched, paid services in other currencies pause rather than guess.
0 = no paid services at all: cloud providers are never called. Limits you set with the provider always apply as well.
Not recommended.
With no limit, spending on cloud services is only capped by what you've set with each provider. A full transcript of a whole library can cost a lot.
Advanced settings
These settings change how results are judged. The defaults are tuned for good results; changing them without understanding the effect can make matches or timing worse.

Costs

For taxes charged on overseas services (such as GST) or your card's foreign-transaction fee, so limits match what you actually pay. Default 0.

Checking

Default 50. The rest are checked on following runs. Each takes a few seconds, longer when speech-to-text is used.
Checks text subtitle tracks inside videos, for languages with no subtitle file beside the video. Copying a track out reads the whole video, so this is off by default and only a few are checked per run. The video is never changed: a track that's out of time gets a corrected copy added beside the video (Undo removes it).
Default 10.
A subtitle in another language than the video's audio (French subtitles for an English film, say) is never compared with what is said. Off, its timing is left as it is and its result says so. On, its timing is checked by when speech starts alone, which works in any language but is less sure; any correction waits for your review. Off by default.
Default 20. Each video counts once for each language it's missing. A search nothing fits is made again after 30 days.
Default 10. Providers such as OpenSubtitles count every download against your account's daily allowance (about 20 on a free OpenSubtitles account), and downloads you make yourself in Jellyfin use the same allowance, so keep this well below it. When the provider says the allowance is used up, the search stops for the day. Up to three candidates are downloaded per video and language, fewer when the first fits; all languages share this limit.

Clean-up

Only overlaps of up to a second; longer ones are usually two people speaking at once and are left alone.
Never into the next line.
Default 500. Short interjections ("Hey!") often run 500–800 ms and read fine; raising this touches many more lines.
Default 833 (5/6 of a second, a common broadcast minimum).
Not recommended: typeset lines are brief and overlap on purpose, and adjusting them breaks signs and karaoke timing.

Judging results

Off by default. The whole-file check ignores words heard with less confidence than a starting point per service (Deepgram 0.90; the built-in, local and OpenAI Whisper services 0.80). With this on, each service and model learns a stricter threshold from subtitles already known to be good (found in sync by speech-to-text, nothing flagged): the lowest at which at most 2 % of their matched words would have been flagged, once 1,000 words have been seen. It only ever raises the threshold, so fewer, surer lines are flagged; it never goes below the starting point.
Not available yet: this setting has no effect in this version.
When speech is heard but the subtitle's words don't match it (a translation, a paraphrase, dense dialogue), the Shoal AI plugin pairs what is said with subtitle lines by meaning; those pairs must still agree on one timing. Sends a few minutes of heard phrases and nearby subtitle lines, nothing else. Only if the AI plugin is installed and allows Subtitles; its spending limits apply.
When a subtitle's timing is settled by speech-to-text, the AI plugin compares its lines with what is said during those few minutes and flags lines whose meaning differs (a wrong name or number, a missing "not", missing words). Suggested wording waits for your review in the results; it is never applied on its own, and Undo brings the original back.
Subtitles checked before the audit existed are audited a few at a time, oldest first, using what is left of the run's AI checks. 0 turns this off.
Each is a fraction of a cent to a few cents, depending on the model.
